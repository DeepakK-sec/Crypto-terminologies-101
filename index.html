01 Cryptography

AES (Advanced Encryption Standard)

A symmetric encryption algorithm widely used to secure data. It encrypts data in fixed 128-bit blocks using keys of 128, 192, or 256 bits, and is the global standard for encrypting sensitive information.

02 Cryptography

Asymmetric Encryption

A cryptographic system that uses a pair of keys: a public key for encryption and a private key for decryption. It enables secure communication without sharing a secret key beforehand.

03 Blockchain & Crypto

Bitcoin

The first decentralized cryptocurrency, created in 2009. It operates on a peer-to-peer network using blockchain technology, allowing secure, borderless transactions without a central authority.

04 Blockchain & Crypto

Blockchain

A distributed, immutable ledger that records transactions in blocks linked together cryptographically. Each block contains a hash of the previous block, making tampering virtually impossible.

05 Cyber Threats & Attacks

Botnet

A network of compromised computers (bots) controlled by an attacker (botmaster). Botnets are used for DDoS attacks, spam distribution, and cryptocurrency mining malware.

06 Cyber Threats & Attacks

Brute Force Attack

A trial-and-error method used to crack passwords or encryption keys by systematically checking all possible combinations until the correct one is found.

07 Security Concepts & Defense

CIA Triad

The three core principles of information security: Confidentiality (only authorized access), Integrity (data accuracy), and Availability (accessible when needed).

08 Blockchain & Crypto

Cryptocurrency

A digital or virtual currency that uses cryptography for security and operates on decentralized networks, typically based on blockchain technology.

09 Cyber Threats & Attacks

Cryptojacking

The unauthorized use of someone's computing resources to mine cryptocurrency. Attackers inject mining scripts into websites or malware to hijack CPU/GPU power.

10 Cyber Threats & Attacks

DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service)

An attack that floods a target server or network with traffic from multiple compromised systems, overwhelming it and making services unavailable to legitimate users.

11 Blockchain & Crypto

Decentralization

The distribution of control and decision-making away from a central authority. In crypto, it means no single entity controls the network.

12 Cryptography

Digital Signature

A cryptographic mechanism that verifies the authenticity and integrity of digital messages or documents, using the sender's private key to sign and public key to verify.

13 Blockchain & Crypto

Double Spending

The risk that a digital currency can be spent twice. Blockchain consensus mechanisms like Proof of Work prevent this by confirming transactions across the network.

14 Cryptography

ECC (Elliptic Curve Cryptography)

A public-key cryptography approach based on the algebraic structure of elliptic curves. It offers strong security with smaller key sizes, making it efficient for mobile and blockchain use.

15 Blockchain & Crypto

Ethereum

A decentralized, open-source blockchain platform that enables smart contracts and decentralized applications (dApps). Its native cryptocurrency is Ether (ETH).

16 Security Concepts & Defense

Firewall

A network security device or software that monitors and filters incoming and outgoing traffic based on predetermined security rules, acting as a barrier between trusted and untrusted networks.

17 Cryptography

Hash Function

A mathematical function that converts input data into a fixed-size string of characters (hash). Cryptographic hashes are one-way, deterministic, and collision-resistant, essential for blockchain integrity.

18 Security Concepts & Defense

Honeypot

A decoy system or resource designed to attract attackers, allowing security teams to study attack methods and detect threats without risking real assets.

19 Security Concepts & Defense

HTTPS (HyperText Transfer Protocol Secure)

An extension of HTTP that uses SSL/TLS encryption to secure communication between a browser and server, protecting data from interception and tampering.

20 Cyber Threats & Attacks

Malware

Malicious software designed to harm, exploit, or gain unauthorized access to systems. Includes viruses, ransomware, spyware, trojans, and crypto-mining malware.

21 Cyber Threats & Attacks

Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) Attack

An attack where the attacker secretly intercepts and possibly alters communication between two parties who believe they are directly communicating with each other.

22 Security Concepts & Defense

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

A security method requiring two or more verification factors (something you know, have, or are) to access an account, significantly reducing unauthorized access risk.

23 Blockchain & Crypto

Nonce (Number Used Once)

A random or semi-random number generated for a specific use in cryptographic communication, often used in mining to find a valid block hash.

24 Cyber Threats & Attacks

Phishing

A social engineering attack where attackers impersonate legitimate entities via email, websites, or messages to steal sensitive information like passwords, private keys, or credit card details.

25 Cryptography

Private Key

A secret cryptographic key used to decrypt data or sign transactions in asymmetric encryption. In crypto, losing your private key means losing access to your funds permanently.

26 Blockchain & Crypto

Proof of Stake (PoS)

A consensus mechanism where validators are chosen to create new blocks based on the amount of cryptocurrency they 'stake' as collateral, more energy-efficient than PoW.

27 Blockchain & Crypto

Proof of Work (PoW)

A consensus mechanism requiring miners to solve complex computational puzzles to validate transactions and create new blocks, securing the network through computational effort.

28 Cryptography

Public Key

A cryptographic key that can be shared openly and is used to encrypt messages or verify digital signatures. It pairs with a private key in asymmetric encryption.

29 Cyber Threats & Attacks

Ransomware

Malicious software that encrypts a victim's files and demands payment (often in cryptocurrency) for the decryption key. Notable examples include WannaCry and NotPetya.

30 Cryptography

RSA (Rivest–Shamir–Adleman)

One of the first public-key cryptosystems, widely used for secure data transmission. Its security relies on the difficulty of factoring large prime numbers.

31 Cryptography

SHA-256 (Secure Hash Algorithm 256-bit)

A cryptographic hash function producing a 256-bit hash value. It's used in Bitcoin's proof-of-work algorithm and for creating Bitcoin addresses.

32 Blockchain & Crypto

Smart Contract

Self-executing contracts with terms written directly into code on a blockchain. They automatically enforce and execute agreements when conditions are met, without intermediaries.

33 Cyber Threats & Attacks

Social Engineering

The manipulation of people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise security, exploiting human psychology rather than technical vulnerabilities.

34 Cryptography

SSL/TLS (Secure Sockets Layer / Transport Layer Security)

Cryptographic protocols that provide secure communication over networks. TLS is the successor to SSL, encrypting data between clients and servers.

35 Blockchain & Crypto

Token

A digital asset created on an existing blockchain (like Ethereum's ERC-20 tokens). Tokens can represent assets, utilities, or access rights within a specific ecosystem.

36 Security Concepts & Defense

VPN (Virtual Private Network)

A service that creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and the internet, masking your IP address and protecting your online activity from surveillance and interception.

37 Blockchain & Crypto

Wallet (Crypto Wallet)

A software, hardware, or paper-based tool that stores private and public keys, allowing users to send, receive, and manage cryptocurrencies. Can be hot (online) or cold (offline).

38 Cyber Threats & Attacks

Zero-Day Exploit

A cyberattack that targets a previously unknown software vulnerability, giving developers 'zero days' to fix it before it's exploited. Highly valuable in cybercrime and espionage.

39 Security Concepts & Defense

Zero Trust Architecture

A security model that requires strict identity verification for every person and device trying to access resources, regardless of whether they're inside or outside the network perimeter.

40 Blockchain & Crypto

51% Attack

An attack on a blockchain where a single entity or group gains control of more than 50% of the network's mining power, allowing them to reverse transactions and double-spend coins.
